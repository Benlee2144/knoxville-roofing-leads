Most roof leaks are small problems that turn into big ones because they go unnoticed. Knoxville averages about 52 inches of rain a year, so a slow drip around a vent pipe or chimney gets plenty of chances to soak insulation, stain ceilings and rot decking. The good news is that many leaks are a straightforward repair once someone finds where the water is actually getting in.

What to do the moment you see a leak

Move furniture and electronics out of the way, put a bucket under the drip, and if a ceiling is sagging with water, keep everyone out from under it. Take dated photos of the stain, the attic if you can safely look, and anything that got wet. Stay off the roof, especially when it's wet.

If rain is on the way and water is coming in steadily, ask roofers about temporary tarping. When you request quotes, choosing 'ASAP / emergency' tells the roofers matched with you that it's urgent.

Where roof leaks usually start

Water rarely comes in through the middle of a healthy shingle. It gets in where the roof is interrupted: flashing around chimneys and where the roof meets a wall, rubber boots around plumbing vents that crack in the sun, valleys where two slopes meet, skylights, and exhaust vents. Shingles lifted or torn by wind open a path too, and in East Tennessee most summer storm damage is wind, according to the National Weather Service.

Clogged gutters and valleys can also push water back under the shingle edges, which looks like a roof leak near the eaves after heavy rain. On wooded lots, leaves are often part of the problem.

Why the stain isn't where the leak is

Water usually runs along the underside of the decking or down a rafter before it drips, so the real entry point is often a few feet uphill from the ceiling stain. A good roofer checks the attic and the roof above the stain, tests the likely spots, and explains what they found before they fix it. Ask them to photograph the source and the repair.

What leak repair costs, and when it's time to replace

HomeAdvisor's national data puts roof leak repairs around $360 to $1,550, and flashing repairs around $200 to $500. Simple fixes like a new pipe boot sit at the low end. If the leak has rotted the decking, or the flashing has to be rebuilt, expect more, and ask for that change in writing.

One leak on a sound roof is a repair. Several leaks on a roof that's 20 or more years old usually means the roof is near the end of its life, and it's worth getting a replacement quote at the same time so you can compare.

Before you sign: checklist

  • Take dated photos of the stain, attic and any damage
  • Ask the roofer to show you where the water was getting in
  • Get the repair, materials and any decking work in writing
  • Ask whether clogged gutters or valleys played a part
  • On a newer roof, check your workmanship warranty before paying
  • If the roof is 20+ years old, ask for a replacement price too

Roof Leak Repair Questions

How much does it cost to fix a roof leak?

HomeAdvisor's national figures put most roof leak repairs around $360 to $1,550. A cracked vent boot is usually at the low end; leaks that need flashing rebuilt or decking replaced cost more. Ask for a written quote that names the source of the leak and exactly what will be repaired.

Can a roofer find a leak if it's not raining?

Often, yes. Roofers look for the usual suspects above the stain, such as flashing, vent boots, valleys and damaged shingles, and check the attic for water trails and stains. Some leaks only show in wind-driven rain, so photos of the leak while it's happening help a lot.

Will insurance cover my roof leak?

It depends on the cause. Homeowners policies usually cover sudden damage, like a tree limb or wind tearing shingles off, but not leaks caused by age or wear. Document the damage, have a roofer write up the cause, and contact your insurer promptly if it looks storm-related.

Should I repair the leak or replace the roof?

If the roof is otherwise in good shape, repair the leak. If it's near the end of its expected life, about 15 to 20 years for 3-tab and 20 to 30 for architectural shingles, or leaks keep appearing in new places, get a replacement quote too and compare the long-term cost.
