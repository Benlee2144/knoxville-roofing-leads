Commercial buildings in the Knoxville area see the same weather as houses, but many sit under flat or low-slope roofs where drains and scuppers, not steep pitch, have to move water off. Rooftop HVAC units add penetrations and foot traffic, and a leak can interrupt a business as well as damage a ceiling. This page covers the main membrane systems, what national guides report for installed cost and expected life, how Tennessee licensing applies to commercial work, and how to compare commercial bids.

Low-slope systems you'll be quoted

TPO is a single-ply membrane, usually white, with heat-welded seams. EPDM is a black synthetic rubber membrane that's typically adhered, mechanically fastened or ballasted, with seams joined by tape or adhesive. Modified bitumen is an asphalt-based sheet applied in layers, and built-up roofing (BUR) alternates layers of felt and asphalt, often finished with gravel. Metal panels are also an option on buildings with enough slope to shed water.

InterNACHI's life-expectancy chart gives broad ranges: 7 to 20 years for TPO, 15 to 25 for EPDM, about 20 for modified bitumen and about 30 for built-up roofing. Membrane thickness, attachment method, insulation and maintenance push a given roof toward one end of its range or the other.

Flat-roof trouble spots in this climate

With about 52 inches of precipitation in a normal year at Knoxville, drainage is the first thing to look at. Water that ponds for days after a rain, clogged drains and gutters, and debris built up around scuppers all shorten a membrane's life. Summer heat and sun also work on seams and flashings, especially around rooftop equipment.

Many commercial leaks start at penetrations: HVAC curbs, pipe stacks, exhaust fans, skylights and parapet walls all rely on flashing details. Technicians walking to rooftop units can puncture a membrane, so ask about walkway pads along regular service routes.

Routine inspections catch many of these problems while they're still small. Building owners often have a commercial roof checked in spring and fall and after major storms, with drains cleared and the flashing at equipment curbs looked over each time. A simple log of leaks, repairs and inspection photos also helps with warranty claims and with budgeting for the eventual replacement.

Repair, restore or replace

A commercial roof doesn't always need a full tear-off. A localized leak can be patched, and a roof with dry, sound insulation may be a candidate for a coating or a recover system, where a new membrane goes over the existing one. Wet insulation is often the deciding factor, because sealing it under a new roof causes problems later. Ask each roofer how they'll check for it; infrared scans and core cuts are common methods.

When full replacement is due, the job typically includes removing the old membrane and any wet insulation, adding new insulation and cover board, and installing new membrane, edge metal and flashing. Work hours, staging areas, equipment disconnects and tenant notices belong in the plan.

Licensing and bidding in Tennessee

Tennessee requires a state contractor's license before anyone bids or negotiates a price on a project of $25,000 or more, and roofing subcontractors need one when their share of a project reaches $25,000. The Board for Licensing Contractors lists roofing as classification BC-21. Each license carries a monetary limit, so confirm the contractor's limit covers the size of your job.

The state Home Improvement license applies to residential remodeling, so it doesn't cover a storefront or office. For commercial jobs under $25,000, TDCI suggests checking with your local building codes office about license and permit requirements. Ask every bidder for a certificate of insurance listing your company as the certificate holder.

Comparing commercial roofing bids

Commercial bids can look alike and still differ a lot. Line them up by membrane type and thickness (for example, 45-mil versus 60-mil TPO), attachment method, insulation thickness and R-value, cover board, tapered insulation for drainage, edge metal, and how many drains and curbs are being reflashed.

Read the warranty terms closely. A manufacturer's system warranty, a materials-only warranty and the contractor's own workmanship warranty are different documents with different conditions. Ask what the manufacturer requires in inspections and maintenance to keep coverage in force.

Before you sign: checklist

  • Confirm the contractor's Tennessee license, classification and monetary limit at search.cloud.commerce.tn.gov
  • Request a certificate of insurance naming your company as certificate holder
  • Ask how wet insulation will be found and how replacing it is priced
  • Compare membrane type, thickness and attachment method across bids
  • Ask which drains, curbs and penetrations will be reflashed
  • Get the manufacturer warranty type, length and maintenance requirements
  • Agree on work hours, staging areas and how rooftop equipment will be handled

Commercial Roofing Questions

How long does a commercial flat roof last?

InterNACHI estimates 7 to 20 years for TPO, 15 to 25 for EPDM, about 20 for modified bitumen and about 30 for built-up roofing. Thicker membranes, good drainage and regular inspections, especially around rooftop equipment, help a roof reach the upper end. Ponding water and neglected penetrations pull it toward the lower end.

Can a new roof go over our existing commercial roof?

Sometimes. A recover system or coating can work when the existing roof and insulation are dry and sound, and it avoids the cost and disruption of a full tear-off. If moisture is trapped in the insulation, covering it usually causes problems later. Ask bidders to explain how they tested for moisture and what codes allow for your building.

What license does a commercial roofer need in Tennessee?

For any project of $25,000 or more, Tennessee requires a state contractor's license before bidding, and roofing subcontractors need one when their portion reaches $25,000. Roofing is classification BC-21. Licenses carry a monetary limit, so check that it covers your job. For smaller commercial jobs, ask your local codes office about local licensing.

Is requesting quotes free for businesses?

Yes. There's no charge to request quotes and no obligation to hire. Local roofing contractors pay for the referral, and you'll hear from up to 3 licensed, insured roofers, usually within 24 to 48 hours. Describing the building, roof type and any active leaks in your request helps them prepare.
