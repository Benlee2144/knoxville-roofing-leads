Lenoir City sits immediately north of Fort Loudoun Dam, which TVA completed in 1943, and the Tennessee River forms its southern boundary. The town grew on land that was once the Lenoir family's plantation, where William Ballard Lenoir built a cotton mill in the early 1830s, and it was incorporated in 1907. Most of the city is in Loudon County, with a small part in Roane County. Interstate 75 and US 70 cross US 321 on the north side of town.

Mill-town streets near downtown

The Lenoir City Company put up its office building downtown in 1890, and that building, now home to the Lenoir City Museum, is on the National Register. The Lenoir Car Works, once the largest business in Loudon County, covered ninety-three acres along the river, and it and a knitting mill each employed several hundred workers in 1910.

Homes on the older streets near downtown can hide original board decking, multiple shingle layers and chimneys that need new flashing or repointing. Ask for a per-sheet price to replace damaged decking, so the quote covers whatever a tear-off uncovers.

Living on the lakes

Fort Loudoun Lake covers about 14,600 acres behind the dam, and a short canal links it to Tellico Lake on the Little Tennessee River. Lake lots along roads such as Lakeland Drive face open water, and wind crossing a wide stretch of lake can hit those roofs harder than it hits sheltered houses inland.

For a lakefront house, ask about the shingle's wind rating, sealed starter strips and corrosion-resistant fasteners and flashing. On a steep lot that drops toward the water, the roofer also needs a plan for getting materials down and debris out without damaging the yard, septic area or dock.

Newer homes along US 321

Much of the newer building in Lenoir City has gone up along US 321 and the Town Creek area, where the Town Creek Parkway project tied Harrison Road into the highway.

A newer roof rarely needs replacing for age, but storms and installation mistakes still cause leaks. Missing drip edge, poorly sealed pipe boots and nails placed too high are common culprits, so if a young roof is leaking, ask a roofer to check workmanship before assuming the shingles failed.

Between Knox and Blount counties

Until 2017, US 321 crossed the river on a bridge atop Fort Loudoun Dam; a new bridge just southeast of the dam now carries Lamar Alexander Parkway toward Maryville, while US 70 runs northeast to Farragut. When quotes come in, ask each roofer to break out tear-off, underlayment, roofing material, flashing, ventilation and cleanup, plus any extra charge for steep or hard-to-reach lake lots, so the numbers line up side by side.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Roofing Questions from Lenoir City Homeowners

Is Tellico Village part of Lenoir City?

No. Tellico Village is a separate census-designated community in Loudon County, and it has its own page on this site. Lenoir City addresses use ZIP codes 37771 and 37772. Wherever your Loudon County home is, either page will connect you with up to 3 local roofers at no cost.

Does Loudon County require a home improvement license for roofers?

Loudon County is not on the list of nine counties where Tennessee's Home Improvement license applies. A state contractor's license is still required for any job of $25,000 or more. On a smaller job, ask for proof of liability insurance, a written contract that spells out the scope, and a few local references you can actually call.

Can roofers get materials to a lake house that sits below the road?

Usually, but access affects the price and the schedule. Steep driveways and lots that fall toward the water can mean hand-carrying materials, using a conveyor or bringing a smaller dumpster. Mention the access when you request quotes, and ask each roofer how they will protect the lawn, septic area and any dock or seawall.
