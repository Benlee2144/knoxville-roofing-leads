A metal roof is a long-term decision. InterNACHI's life-expectancy chart puts metal roofing at 40 to 80 years, compared with roughly 20 to 30 for asphalt shingles, and the price reflects that. With spring hail, summer thunderstorm winds and humid summers in the Knoxville area, it's worth weighing metal against architectural shingles when your current roof wears out. This page compares standing seam and exposed-fastener systems, lists what 2026 cost guides report, and covers what to ask an installer.

Standing seam vs. exposed-fastener panels

Standing seam panels run from ridge to eave with raised seams that lock or snap together, and they're held down by clips or fasteners concealed under the seam. No screws pierce the face of the roof. The clips let panels expand and contract with temperature, which matters on a roof that swings between freezing winter nights and hot summer afternoons.

Exposed-fastener panels, often called screw-down, ribbed or corrugated panels, are fastened through the face with screws that carry rubber washers. They cost less to buy and install, but every screw is a potential leak point. Over the years washers can break down and screws can back out, so plan on periodic inspection and refastening.

A third option, metal shingles or shakes, is stamped to resemble asphalt, slate or wood while keeping the durability of metal. HomeAdvisor lists them at $7 to $22 per square foot installed. They can suit a street or homeowners association where a panel look would stand out.

What metal roofing costs

Guides published in 2026 give wide ranges because metal type, gauge, panel profile and roof complexity all move the price. Modernize's Tennessee guide reports $9 to $18 per square foot installed for most metal systems in the state, and $22,000 to $40,000 to put standing seam on a 2,000 square foot roof. Exposed-fastener panels come in lower; Roof Observations' 2026 steel guide puts them around $8.25 per square foot installed on average, versus about $12.75 for standing seam steel.

Steep pitches, multiple valleys and dormers, skylights and chimneys all add labor and trim pieces. The same panel can cost noticeably more on a complicated roof than on a simple gable.

National figures run even wider. HomeAdvisor's June 2026 data lists standing seam at $7 to $30 per square foot and corrugated sheets at $3.50 to $15. Ranges that broad are a reminder to compare quotes on the same panel, gauge and finish rather than on price per square alone.

How metal handles East Tennessee weather

Hail can dent metal panels. Some homeowners policies treat dents on metal as cosmetic and limit coverage for them, so ask your agent how your policy handles it before you choose a panel. Heavier-gauge metal resists denting better than thinner panels.

Moisture is the other consideration. Knoxville's average morning relative humidity runs around 85 percent, and condensation can form on the underside of metal panels when warm, damp air meets a cold surface. A suitable underlayment and good attic ventilation are part of a sound metal installation, not optional extras.

Installation details to ask about

Ask whether panels will go over solid decking with underlayment or over purlins, what gauge and paint system are being quoted, and whether the finish carries its own warranty against fading and chalking. On standing seam, ask whether panels are roll-formed on site or delivered pre-cut, and how long runs are allowed to move.

Trim and flashing are where metal roofs succeed or fail. Ridge caps, valleys, eave and rake trim, pipe boots and chimney flashing should be designed for the specific panel system. A crew that's excellent with shingles may have less experience with metal, so ask how many metal roofs they've installed and request addresses you can drive past.

Comparing metal roofing quotes

Make sure each quote names the panel profile, metal (steel or aluminum), gauge, finish system and color, along with the underlayment, fastener type and trim package. Two quotes that both say standing seam can describe very different products.

Licensing still applies. At the standing seam prices in Modernize's Tennessee guide, many full installations will reach $25,000, the point where Tennessee requires a state contractor's license. Below that, residential metal roofing jobs in Knox County from $3,000 to $24,999 fall under the state Home Improvement license.

Before you sign: checklist

  • Get the panel profile, metal type and gauge in writing
  • Ask what paint system is used and whether it has its own fade and chalk warranty
  • Confirm the underlayment product and that it's suitable for use under metal
  • Ask how panel expansion is handled on long runs
  • Find out how the crew will flash chimneys, skylights and sidewalls
  • Ask your insurance agent how hail dents on metal are covered
  • Request addresses of metal roofs the contractor has installed
  • Verify the Tennessee license that matches the contract price

COMMON QUESTIONS

Metal Roofing Questions

How long does a metal roof last?

InterNACHI's life-expectancy chart estimates 40 to 80 years for metal roofing, and HomeAdvisor cites 40 to 70 years for a well-installed metal roof. Actual life depends on the metal, the paint system, installation quality and upkeep. Exposed-fastener roofs need their screws and washers checked over time, while standing seam systems need less routine attention.

Does a metal roof cost more than shingles?

Yes, at the start. Modernize's 2026 Tennessee guide lists architectural shingles at $5 to $10.50 per square foot installed and most metal systems at $9 to $18. Over the life of the house the comparison narrows, because a metal roof can outlast two or more shingle roofs. How long you plan to stay in the home is a big part of the math.

Can metal go over my existing shingles?

Some installers will put metal over one layer of shingles where codes and the panel manufacturer allow it, often with battens or an added underlayment layer, while others insist on a tear-off. Ask each installer which method they're quoting, how it affects ventilation, and what the manufacturer requires to keep the warranty valid.

Standing seam or exposed fastener: which is better for a house?

Standing seam costs more but has no exposed screws and allows the panels to move with temperature changes, which reduces long-term maintenance. Exposed-fastener panels are the budget option and can work well on simpler roofs if you're prepared to inspect and replace fasteners over time. Get both priced for your roof before deciding.
