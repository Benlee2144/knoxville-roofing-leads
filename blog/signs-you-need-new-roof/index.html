8 Warning Signs You Need a New Roof

Updated October 2026 · 5 min read · By the Knoxville Roofing Quotes team

Nobody wakes up excited to spend money on a new roof. But ignoring roof problems doesn't make them go away; it usually makes them worse and more expensive.

Here are eight warning signs that your roof may be near the end. Some are obvious. Others, not so much.

1. Your Roof Is Just Plain Old

Start with age. Based on Modernize and InterNACHI estimates, 3-tab asphalt shingles typically last about 15 to 20 years and architectural shingles about 20 to 30. InterNACHI notes that hot climates and severe weather like hail can shorten those numbers. Our guide to how long roofs last in Tennessee has the details by material.

Check your records, the home inspection from when you bought the house, or ask the previous owners. If nobody knows, a roofer can usually estimate age from the wear. Wondering what replacement might cost? See our Knoxville roof replacement cost guide.

2. Shingles Are Curling or Buckling

Walk outside and look at your roof. Do the shingles lay flat like they should? Or are the edges curling up like old book pages? Maybe the middle is bubbling up?

Curled shingles let wind-driven rain get underneath, and they're easier for wind to lift or tear off. Around Knoxville, the National Weather Service says most summer severe weather is wind damage, so curled shingles are a weak point when storms roll through.

3. You're Finding Granules in the Gutters

Those little sandy bits in your gutters aren't dirt – they're granules from your shingles. Some shedding is normal, especially on newer roofs. But if you're scooping out handfuls? That's a problem.

Granules protect the asphalt from the sun. Once a lot of them are gone, shingles age faster. Heavy granule loss on an older roof is a sign it's wearing out; sudden granule piles after a storm can point to hail damage, which our hail damage guide covers.

4. Daylight Coming Through the Attic

Go up to your attic on a sunny day. Turn off the lights. Look at the roof deck. See any light coming through? That's not good.

If light can get in, so can water. Also look for water stains on the rafters and the underside of the deck, damp insulation, and rusty nail tips. Those point to leaks or condensation you can't see from outside. (Light along a ridge vent is normal; light through the deck itself isn't.)

5. The Roof Is Sagging

Stand across the street and look at your roofline. It should be straight. If it's dipping in the middle or sagging anywhere, you've got structural issues.

Sagging often means the decking or framing underneath has been weakened, frequently by long-term moisture. This isn't a "maybe next year" situation. Get it checked soon, and stay off that part of the roof.

6. Your Attic Runs Hot, Damp or Drafty

A roof that's struggling often shows it in the attic first: musty smells, frost or condensation on the underside of the deck in winter, or an attic that's far hotter than outside in summer. Those usually point to ventilation or insulation problems, which can shorten the life of the shingles and, under many manufacturer warranties, affect coverage. ENERGY STAR says sealing air leaks and adding insulation can save up to 10% on annual energy bills, so it's worth checking both while you're up there. See our guides to roof ventilation and attic insulation.

7. Moss or Algae Growth

A little green isn't always a crisis. But heavy moss holds moisture against your shingles and can lift them. Those black streaks you see on so many East Tennessee roofs? That's usually algae, and it's mostly cosmetic; our guide to black streaks on roofs explains what to do about it.

The bigger concern is what moss growth tells you – your roof isn't drying properly. Could be poor drainage, could be too much shade, could be failing shingles that stay damp too long.

8. You've Already Had Multiple Repairs

Sometimes repairs become throwing good money after bad. If you're patching the same aging roof every year or two, the money might be better spent toward a replacement. HomeAdvisor's June 2026 data puts most roof repairs between about $395 and $1,968 nationally, and those bills add up on a roof near the end of its life.

At some point, a full replacement is actually the cheaper option. A good roofer will be honest about when you've crossed that line.

What Now?

If you recognized your roof in any of these signs, don't panic. Get a professional inspection. Many roofers include an inspection free when they quote a repair or replacement; some charge for a stand-alone inspection or a detailed report, so ask up front. Our roof inspection page explains what a good one includes.

Maybe it's fine and you've got a few more years. Maybe you need some repairs. Or maybe it's time for a new roof. Either way, knowing is better than guessing.

One More Thing

If you're getting quotes, get more than one. Prices can vary a lot for the same roof, depending on materials, scope and the company.
