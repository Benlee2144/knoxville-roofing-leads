How Long Does a Roof Replacement Actually Take?

Updated October 2026 · 4 min read · By the Knoxville Roofing Quotes team

You've signed the contract and the materials are ordered. Now you're wondering how long your house is going to be a construction zone.

The short answer: Angi's roofing guide says a standard asphalt shingle roof usually takes one to three days to replace, and roofs of other materials can take up to about 12 days. The longer answer depends on your roof, the weather and the crew.

Typical Timelines

Simple asphalt shingle roof: often a single day

Typical asphalt shingle roof: 1-3 days

Large or complex roof (dormers, valleys, steep pitch): longer

Metal, tile or slate: longer still, up to about 12 days per Angi

Size is part of it. Roofers price and plan by the "square," which is 100 square feet of roof. HomeAdvisor's rule of thumb is that a roof covers about 1.1 to 1.2 times the home's square footage, depending on its shape and slope, so a 2,000-square-foot single-story home has roughly 22 to 24 squares of roof. Your quote should list the number of squares; our quote comparison worksheet helps you line them up.

What Can Slow Things Down

Weather

This is the big one. Roofers don't want to tear off a roof with rain on the way, and shingle manufacturers add cold-weather precautions at or below 40°F. Around Knoxville, the National Weather Service says severe storms peak in the afternoon, around 4 p.m., and spring is the heart of storm season. Good crews watch the forecast and make sure the roof is dried in before rain arrives. Our guide to the best time of year to replace a roof in Knoxville covers the seasons month by month.

Roof Complexity

A simple gable roof goes quickly. Multiple levels, dormers, valleys, skylights, chimneys and steep pitches all take longer, because every penetration needs flashing and every valley needs careful detailing. Steep roofs also need more safety setup.

Deck Damage

Nobody knows for sure what's under the shingles until they come off. Rotted or soft decking has to be replaced before new roofing goes on. Ask each roofer up front how they price deck replacement so there are no surprises.

Multiple Layers

Some older homes have two layers of shingles because someone roofed over the old roof. Tearing off more layers takes longer and creates more debris.

Crew Size

A bigger crew works faster. Ask how many people will be on your job and whether it's the company's own crew or a subcontractor.

A Typical Two-Day Job

Here's roughly how a two-day asphalt shingle replacement goes:

Day 1

  • Early morning: The crew arrives and sets up. Tarps go down around the house to catch debris.
  • Morning: Tear-off. This is the loud part. Old shingles go into a dumpster or trailer.
  • Midday: Deck inspection, and any damaged wood gets replaced.
  • Afternoon: Drip edge, ice and water barrier in vulnerable areas like valleys and eaves, then underlayment over the deck.
  • End of day: The roof is dried in, protected from rain though not finished.

Day 2

  • Morning: Starter strips along the edges, then the main shingles.
  • Midday: Flashing at walls, chimneys and pipes; vents installed.
  • Afternoon: Ridge cap shingles and final details.
  • Late afternoon: Cleanup, including a magnetic sweep for nails, and haul-away.

The Whole Process, Start to Finish

The one to three days of work is only part of it:

  • Getting quotes: Allow time to meet with two or three roofers and compare written quotes
  • Contract to start date: Depends on the roofer's backlog, which grows after big storms
  • HOA approval: If your neighborhood requires architectural review, build that in
  • Permit: Ask the roofer whether your job needs one and who will pull it; rules vary by city and county
  • Material delivery: Often a day or two before work starts
  • Installation: Usually one to three days for asphalt shingles
  • Final walk-through: Look the job over with the roofer before the final payment

Can You Stay Home During the Work?

Yes, but it won't be peaceful. Expect:

  • Noise: Tear-off and nail guns, all day
  • Vibration: Wall hangings can shift, so take down anything fragile
  • Dust: Some debris can fall into the attic, so cover anything stored up there
  • Blocked driveway: A dumpster or trailer and work trucks

If you work from home or have small kids or pets, consider making other plans for tear-off day.

How to Keep Things on Schedule

  • Be responsive when the roofer calls about scheduling or color choices.
  • Clear the area. Move patio furniture, grills, potted plants and cars away from the house.
  • Stay flexible. If the crew can start earlier because of a weather window, take it if you can.
  • Don't pay too much up front. TDCI says Tennessee law limits home improvement deposits to one-third of the contract price.

The Bottom Line

Most asphalt shingle replacements take one to three days of actual work, but weather can stretch that, so build some buffer into your plans.
