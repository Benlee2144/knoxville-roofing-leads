Roof Warranties Explained: What's Actually Covered

Updated October 2026 · 4 min read · By the Knoxville Roofing Quotes team

"Lifetime warranty" sounds great. But what does it actually mean on a roof? Warranty language is confusing, and coverage that looks solid can have big gaps. Here's how roof warranties work, using a real example: GAF's standard shingle warranty, since GAF is one of the largest shingle makers. Other manufacturers' terms differ, so always read the one for your shingles.

Two Types of Warranties

A new roof comes with two separate warranties:

1. Manufacturer Warranty

This covers defects in the materials themselves. It doesn't cover installation mistakes; GAF's warranty, for example, excludes damage caused by installation that didn't follow its instructions.

2. Workmanship Warranty

This comes from your roofer and covers how the roof was installed: nailing, flashing, ventilation and details. If the shingles are fine but the installation wasn't, this is what protects you. Length and terms vary a lot from company to company.

You need both. Great shingles installed badly can leak, and the manufacturer won't pay for an installation problem.

What "Lifetime" Actually Means

Reality check: In GAF's warranty, "Lifetime" means as long as you, the original owner, own the home, or the second owner if coverage was properly transferred during the early full-coverage period. It doesn't mean full coverage forever.

Here's how GAF's coverage for its Lifetime shingles on a single-family home is structured:

  • First 10 years (the "Smart Choice Protection Period"): non-prorated. GAF pays the reasonable cost of labor to repair or re-cover defective products and provides replacement materials. It doesn't pay for tear-off or disposal.
  • After that: labor isn't covered, and the material reimbursement is reduced for the years you've had the roof. GAF's own example: a claim at 25 years is reduced by 50%.
  • Year 41 and beyond: GAF's contribution is 20%.

That's why the fine print matters more than the headline. The coverage schedule tells you what you'd actually get.

What's Usually Covered

Under the Manufacturer Warranty

  • Manufacturing defects that affect performance or cause leaks
  • Wind damage up to the shingle's rated wind speed, if installed to the manufacturer's instructions
  • Algae discoloration on algae-resistant shingles, for a set number of years

Under the Workmanship Warranty

  • Leaks caused by improper installation
  • Flashing failures due to poor work
  • Improperly sealed penetrations
  • Problems from incorrect nailing

Ask exactly what the roofer's warranty covers, for how long, and whether it's in writing.

What's Usually NOT Covered

GAF's warranty lists exclusions that are typical of the industry:

  • Storm damage: hail, fire and wind above the rated speed. That's a homeowners insurance matter, not a warranty claim. See our insurance claim guide.
  • Inadequate ventilation: damage caused by poor attic ventilation isn't covered. Our ventilation guide explains why it matters.
  • Installation problems: damage from installation that didn't follow the manufacturer's instructions
  • Cold-weather sealing: GAF notes shingles installed in fall or winter may not seal until spring, and excludes failures to hand-seal shingles installed in cold weather
  • Building movement: settling or structural problems in the house or roof deck
  • Foot traffic and impacts: including objects blown onto the roof
  • Consequential damage: water damage to the inside of your home
  • Tear-off and disposal costs

Selling Your Home

Transfer rules matter if you might move. GAF's standard warranty can be transferred only once, and the new owner has to notify GAF in writing within one year of buying the home. If the transfer happens during the first 10 years, the new owner gets the same coverage; after that, coverage drops to two years. Check your manufacturer's rules and keep the paperwork for the next owner.

Enhanced Warranties

Manufacturers offer stronger warranties when the roof is installed by a contractor they've certified and the system uses their matching components. These can extend the non-prorated period or add some workmanship coverage backed by the manufacturer. They may require registration and sometimes cost extra. Ask each roofer which certifications they hold and get the exact warranty document in writing before you sign.

Questions to Ask Your Roofer

  • What exactly does your workmanship warranty cover, and for how long?
  • Which manufacturer warranty comes with these shingles, and how long is the full-coverage period?
  • Does the warranty need to be registered, and who does that?
  • Will the installation meet the manufacturer's ventilation and installation requirements?
  • Does it transfer if I sell the house?
  • Can I see the warranty documents before I sign?

Our 12 questions to ask before hiring a roofer covers the rest of the conversation.

Keep Your Documentation

Years from now, you may need:

  • The roofer's written workmanship warranty
  • The manufacturer warranty and any registration confirmation
  • Your contract and proof of payment
  • The exact products installed (brand, line and color)
  • The installation date

Store them somewhere you'll find them, along with a few photos of the finished roof.

What Actually Matters

  1. Installation quality: manufacturer warranties exclude installation problems, so the roofer matters most
  2. The roofer's staying power: a workmanship warranty is only as good as the company behind it
  3. The full-coverage period: the years with labor included are the most valuable part of a manufacturer warranty
  4. Everything in writing: verbal promises aren't warranties
