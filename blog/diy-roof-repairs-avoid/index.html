DIY Roof Repairs: What to Try vs What to Avoid

Updated October 2026 · 4 min read · By the Knoxville Roofing Quotes team

Online videos make roof work look easy. But roofing is one area where DIY can go wrong fast: making a leak worse, causing problems with a warranty, or ending with a trip to the ER.

Here's how to think about what's reasonable to handle yourself and what's better left to a licensed roofer.

What You Can Usually Do Yourself

These are generally manageable if you're handy and comfortable on a ladder, ideally from the ground or the ladder rather than on the roof:

Cleaning Gutters

Use a sturdy ladder, have someone hold it, and wear gloves. Don't overreach; climb down and move the ladder instead. Our gutter maintenance guide covers how often to clean them under Knoxville's trees.

Removing Debris

Leaves and small branches that collect in valleys and behind chimneys hold moisture against the shingles. A roof rake used from the ground handles most of it. Have a tree service deal with large limbs over the roof.

Looking Things Over

You can spot a lot from the ground with binoculars and from inside the attic with a flashlight. Our roof inspection checklist walks through what to look for.

Temporary Protection After Damage

If a storm opens a hole and it's safe to reach, a tarp can limit water damage until a roofer arrives. Read our emergency roof tarp guide first, and don't climb onto a wet or damaged roof.

What to Avoid

These tasks carry a real risk of getting hurt or making the problem worse:

Working on Steep Roofs

OSHA's construction rules treat any roof steeper than 4 in 12 (4 inches of rise per 12 inches of run) as a steep roof, and many Knoxville homes are steeper than that. Professional crews use fall protection, roof jacks and experience with footing. Falls are the leading cause of death in construction, according to OSHA, and that's among people who work at heights for a living.

Flashing Repair

Flashing looks simple but isn't. Pieces have to overlap in the right order to shed water, and a wrong repair can send water somewhere new. The leak might not show up until the next heavy rain, after you think it's fixed. See our chimney flashing guide for what a proper repair involves.

Anything Structural

If you see rotted decking, sagging, or soft spots, stop. That needs a professional, and walking on a weakened deck is dangerous.

Replacing Sections of Shingles

The layering, nailing pattern and tie-in with existing shingles are more involved than they look, and shingle manufacturers' warranties have installation requirements. A bad repair can also make it harder to show storm damage to an insurer later.

Anything When It's Wet, Icy or Windy

Roofs are slick when wet or frosty, even low-slope ones. Wait for dry, calm conditions, or call a roofer.

Work Near Power Lines

Look up before you raise a ladder. Contact with overhead power lines can be fatal, and metal ladders conduct electricity. If a line runs near the roof, leave the job to a pro.

The Hidden Costs of DIY Mistakes

  • Warranty trouble: Manufacturer warranties have installation requirements, so read yours before altering the roof.
  • Insurance questions: If a DIY repair fails and causes damage, ask your insurer how that would be handled before you start.
  • Making it worse: A wrong repair can let water travel and cause damage in places that are harder to fix.
  • Hidden damage: What looks like a simple shingle issue can turn out to be rotted decking.

If You Do Go Up There

  • Tell someone, and don't work alone.
  • Wear soft-soled shoes with good grip.
  • Set the ladder correctly. OSHA's construction standard requires ladder rails to extend at least 3 feet above the edge you're stepping onto. Set it on firm, level ground and tie it off if you can.
  • Stay back from edges.
  • Work in good conditions: dry, calm and not too hot. Summer afternoons in Knoxville are hard on people and soften shingles.
  • Know your limits. If it feels sketchy, come down.

When to Just Call

If you're watching videos to figure out how to do it, it's probably a job for a pro. Roofers have the equipment, the insurance and the experience to do it right the first time. Many small repairs cost less than people expect: HomeAdvisor's June 2026 national data puts most roof repairs between about $395 and $1,968. Our roof repair page explains what to expect.
