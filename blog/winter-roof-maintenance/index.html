Winter Roof Maintenance Tips for Knoxville

Updated October 2026 · 4 min read · By the Knoxville Roofing Quotes team

Knoxville winters aren't brutal like up north, but don't let that fool you. The area averages about 68 nights a year at or below freezing, according to the National Weather Service, so water that gets into cracks and gaps freezes, expands and thaws again many times a winter.

A little maintenance before winter hits can save you from a lot of problems later. Here's what to do.

Before Winter: The Pre-Season Checklist

Do these in late October/November:

  • Clean gutters and downspouts
  • Trim overhanging branches
  • Check flashing around chimneys and vents
  • Look for damaged or missing shingles
  • Check attic ventilation
  • Inspect attic insulation

Clean Those Gutters

Nobody likes cleaning gutters. But fall leaves clog them right before winter rain, and blocked gutters push water back under the roof edge and into the fascia. In a freeze, a clogged gutter can also fill with ice.

Clean them after the leaves drop and before the first hard freeze, and make sure downspouts drain away from the foundation. Our gutter maintenance guide has more.

Trim Back Trees

Branches hanging over your roof cause problems:

  • They drop leaves constantly (clogging gutters)
  • Ice-weighted branches can break and fall on your roof
  • They provide critter highways to your attic
  • Shade keeps your roof damp longer

Have limbs that hang over or rub the roof cut back. If big limbs are involved, hire a tree service. This isn't a ladder-and-chainsaw job.

Check the Flashing

Flashing is the metal around chimneys, vents, skylights and walls. It's one of the most common places leaks start. Our chimney flashing guide explains what a proper repair looks like.

Look for:

  • Lifted or bent flashing
  • Rust spots
  • Gaps in the sealant
  • Missing pieces

If anything looks off, get it fixed before winter. Small flashing repairs are cheap. Water damage is not.

Look for Obvious Shingle Problems

You don't need to climb on the roof. Just walk around your house and look up. Use binoculars if you want a closer look. Watch for:

  • Missing shingles (obvious)
  • Curled or buckled shingles
  • Shingles that look different from the rest (could be storm damage)
  • Dark streaks or moss growth

Get problems fixed before winter if you can. Roofing still happens in cold weather, but shingle manufacturers call for extra care at or below 40°F, and shingles installed in the cold may not seal until warmer weather. Our guide to the best time to replace a roof in Knoxville covers the seasons.

Attic Ventilation and Insulation

This is the stuff people forget about. Ice dams start with heat escaping from the house into the attic, which warms the roof deck. Snow melts, runs down, and refreezes at the colder roof edge.

The fix is a cold roof: air sealing and insulation keep household heat out of the attic, and ventilation carries away what gets through. Check that insulation isn't blocking the soffit vents and that exhaust vents are clear. Our guides to attic insulation and roof ventilation go deeper.

During Winter: What to Watch For

Ice Dams

Knoxville averages only about 4.6 inches of snow a year, so ice dams are uncommon here. But after a heavy snow, big icicles or a ridge of ice at the roof edge mean one may be forming.

What NOT to do: don't hack at ice with a hammer or shovel. You'll damage your shingles and gutters.

What TO do: from the ground, use a roof rake to pull snow off the lower few feet of the roof. Travelers, the insurer, suggests filling a nylon stocking with calcium chloride ice melt and laying it across the dam to melt a drainage channel, and warns not to use rock salt, which can damage your roof. Cover shrubs below, since the runoff can hurt plants. Only do this if you can reach safely; ladders are dangerous in snow and ice. For serious ice dams, call a professional.

Heavy Snow

Knoxville rarely gets deep snow, but a heavy, wet snowfall can stress an older roof, especially one with existing sagging or rot. If heavy snow is sitting on the roof for days, consider raking off the lower edge.

Use a roof rake (long-handled tool specifically for this) from the ground. Never climb on a snowy or icy roof.

Interior Signs of Trouble

Check your attic after major weather events. Look for:

  • Wet insulation
  • Water stains on the underside of the deck
  • Frost on the inside of the roof (sign of too much moisture)
  • Daylight coming through (you shouldn't see any)

In your living space, watch ceilings and exterior walls for new stains or bubbling paint.

After Winter: Spring Check

Once winter breaks, do another walkthrough. Look for:

  • Shingles that got damaged over winter
  • Flashing that shifted with freeze-thaw cycles
  • Gutters that might have been bent by ice
  • Any new stains inside the house

Late winter is a good time for a professional inspection, before spring storm season. Our spring roof checklist covers the rest.

The Easy Version

If you want to keep it simple: clean your gutters, make sure your attic is properly ventilated, and get a professional inspection once a year. Do that, and you'll catch most problems before they become expensive.

An ounce of prevention really is worth a pound of cure, especially when the cure involves opening up a ceiling.
