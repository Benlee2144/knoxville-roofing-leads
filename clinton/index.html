Clinton is the county seat of Anderson County, founded in 1801 as Burrville and renamed in 1809. The city sits on the Clinch River, along a stretch that is technically part of Melton Hill Lake, and it is ringed by long, narrow ridges. Downtown centers on Main and Market streets, while US 25W and State Route 61 lead out toward Knoxville, Norris and Interstate 75. The 2020 census counted 10,056 residents.

Downtown's historic buildings

Clinton became an accredited Tennessee Main Street community in 2021, and its historic downtown runs along Main and Market streets. The oldest remaining building in the district, at 320 N. Main St., dates to the 1890s, and five downtown buildings that shared a state improvement grant in 2022 were built between 1890 and 1940. Market Street reopened in July 2026 after a $9.9 million rebuild of its utilities, sidewalks and lighting.

Many storefronts of that era have low-slope roofs behind parapet walls, and the flashing, wall caps and drains along those parapets need careful inspection. Downtown building owners should mark their request as commercial so the roofers know what they are bidding on.

Homes on the ridges

Clinton's residential streets climb the ridges around downtown and spread out along Charles G. Seivers Boulevard, Dutch Valley Road and the South Clinton area. On wooded lots, leaves pile up in gutters and valleys, and limbs over the roof need trimming before they become storm damage.

On a house built into a hillside, ask how the roofer will stage materials and the dumpster, and whether protecting the driveway and lawn is included in the price.

What to check before you hire

Anderson County never opted into Tennessee's Home Improvement licensing program, so for jobs under $25,000, insurance, references and a clear contract are your main protections. For larger jobs, ask for the roofer's Tennessee contractor's license number.

Get the scope in writing: tear-off, underlayment, flashing, ventilation, cleanup and a per-sheet price for any decking that has to be replaced, along with the warranty terms. That way an unexpected rotten board does not turn into an argument halfway through the job.

Along the Clinch River

South of downtown, the city's 370-acre Aspire park opened in October 2024, and the former Magnet Mills site beside the river is a 7-acre redevelopment property. The Clinch was once known for its freshwater pearls, a trade that faded after Melton Hill Dam changed the river.

If your house sits on low ground near the river or a creek, keep gutters clear and downspouts discharging well away from the foundation, and ask the roofer to confirm the attic is venting moist air out instead of trapping it.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Roofing Questions from Clinton Homeowners

Is Lake City the same place as Rocky Top?

Yes. The Anderson County town north of Clinton began as Coal Creek, became Lake City in 1936 after Norris Dam created Norris Lake, and was renamed Rocky Top in 2014. Some older records, and the post office for ZIP code 37769, may still say Lake City. Homeowners there can request roofing quotes through this page.

I own a building downtown. Can I get commercial roofing quotes?

Yes. Choose the commercial option on the form and describe the building, including whether the roof sits behind a parapet and roughly how large it is. Older buildings on Main and Market streets can have several roof layers from past repairs, so ask each roofer to check what is underneath before quoting a full replacement.

How should I compare quotes from Clinton roofers?

Line the quotes up by scope, not just the total. Check that each covers tear-off, underlayment, ice and water barrier in the valleys, flashing, ventilation, decking replacement pricing and cleanup. On hillside homes, access and decking condition can change the final price, so make sure those lines are spelled out the same way in every quote.
