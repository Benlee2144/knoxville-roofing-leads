Maryville is the county seat of Blount County, incorporated in 1795 and named for Mary Grainger Blount, wife of territorial governor William Blount. It sits in the foothills of the Great Smoky Mountains, with Chilhowee Mountain rising to the south and its twin city, Alcoa, along its northern edge. Maryville College, founded in 1819, anchors the older part of town, and the city had 31,907 residents at the 2020 census.

Historic homes near downtown and the college

South of downtown, the Indiana Avenue Historic District was added to the National Register in 1989. Its nomination counts ninety houses, eighty of them built between about 1880 and 1939, in Late Victorian, Colonial Revival and Craftsman bungalow styles, on about 33 acres roughly bounded by Goddard Street, Court Street, Indiana Avenue and Cates Street. The surrounding College Hill area is a local historic district, and Maryville College has its own National Register listing.

Roofs on houses like these are rarely simple: Victorian homes have hips, gables and dormers, and bungalows have deep overhangs with exposed rafter tails. Ask whether the quote covers repairing fascia, soffits and rafter ends where an old roof leaked, and whether the roofer has worked on homes in a historic district.

Foothills weather

Maryville's July averages run from about 69 to 87 degrees and January's from about 29 to 46. That brings repeated freeze-thaw cycles in winter and long, hot afternoons on dark shingles in summer, which is hard on sealant strips and on attics without enough ventilation.

Homes on open hillsides toward the mountain are more exposed to wind than sheltered lots in town. Ask each roofer about intake and exhaust ventilation and the shingle's wind rating, and make sure both are written into the quote.

Summer heat tests the attic as much as the shingles. A roof deck that runs hot ages shingles faster and raises cooling bills, so ask whether the roofer will balance soffit intake against ridge exhaust instead of simply adding more vents.

Neighborhoods and corridors

Outside the historic core, Maryville spreads along Broadway, Lamar Alexander Parkway (US 321), Montvale Road and William Blount Drive. Broadway, the main street downtown, carries parts of US 411 and US 129. Some subdivisions, including The Park at Royal Oaks, have homeowners associations, so check whether a change in shingle color or material needs approval.

Downtown's look owes a lot to the 1970s Now Town renewal project, which created Bicentennial Greenbelt Park, and many retailers later moved out to Foothills Mall at the junction of US 129 and US 411. Older brick commercial buildings near downtown often have flat or low-slope roofs with internal drains or scuppers. Those call for a roofer who works with commercial membranes, so select the commercial project type when you request quotes.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Roofing Questions from Maryville Homeowners

Does a Maryville roofer need a state license?

For jobs of $25,000 or more, yes, because Tennessee requires a contractor's license in every county. Blount County is not one of the nine counties that adopted the state Home Improvement law, so smaller residential jobs there do not need that separate license. On any job, ask for proof of liability insurance, workers' compensation coverage if the roofer has employees, and a written contract.

What should a roofing contract tied to an insurance claim include?

Tennessee requires a roofer on an insurance-paid job to give you, before you sign, a business address and phone number, any license numbers, a detailed description of the damage and repairs, and a written notice of your right to cancel. That right lets you back out before midnight on the third business day after your insurer says all or part of the claim is not covered.

Is Alcoa part of Maryville?

No. Alcoa is a separate city that borders Maryville to the north, and the two are often called twin cities. Both are in Blount County, and you can request quotes for an address in either one through this page. Maryville addresses use ZIP codes 37801, 37803 and 37804.
